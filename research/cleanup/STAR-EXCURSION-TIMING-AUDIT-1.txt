  For a majority of Stars, any one slice adds nothing, while the Star

  zero is not read as "the move stopped."

  adds nothing after 72. The rising level is the distribution. The
  paired ratio is the typical Star. They are not the same sentence.

  signed gap, exactly. The extreme times do not depend on the entry.
  On this window the median gap is 0.00 price. The two entries do not
  make two different curves here.
- A negative favorable extreme is kept. It means price never went
  beyond the entry. It is not replaced with 0.
- Stars that do not have enough successor bars are left out of that
  window. They are not scored as zero and they are not called timeout.
- The same 15-minute bar can print both extremes. That bar is not
  ordered. Wick time is not a 1-minute barrier race.
- Sides are "up" and "down".
- 94 is not a fifth window. 72 and 96 already bracket that prior.
- No screen was built. There is no frozen TP or SL to draw.

============================================================
4. METHOD
============================================================

Entry for the tables is the Star close (signal).
The next-open fill was computed on the same bars.

up favorable = high − entry. up adverse = entry − low.
down favorable = entry − low. down adverse = high − entry.

MFE is the maximum favorable print in the window.
MAE is the maximum adverse print in the window.
Time is the first bar in that window that prints it.
Bar 1 is the first 15-minute bar after the Star.
16 bars = 4h. 32 = 8h. 72 = 18h. 96 = 24h.

Percentiles are nearest-rank.

The 96-bar ratio is MFE at the shorter window divided by MFE at 96,
for Stars whose 96-bar favorable extreme is positive.
The same for MAE.

7. WHAT THE CURVES SAY
============================================================

Observed:

The typical Star is not finished at 4 hours. About half of its
24-hour favorable extreme is visible by then, and about 70% of
Stars print that extreme later than bar 16.

It is mostly in place by 8 hours: about 80% of the 24-hour
favorable extreme for an up Star, about 86% for a down Star.
By 18 hours the median Star has already printed the favorable
extreme it will have at 24 hours.

The last 6 hours still matter for the right tail, and for the
quarter of Stars whose extreme prints after bar 72.
They do not move the median Star's extreme.

Adverse excursion tracks favorable excursion.
At every window the median heat is about the same ATR size as
the median favorable extreme. The clocks match too.

The population does not show "adverse first, then recovery"
as a majority, and it does not show "favorable first" either.

Fifteen up Stars and nine down Stars never print a positive
favorable extreme inside 96 bars.

Hypothesis, not a rule:

The prior that the meaningful move waits until 72–96 bars is
only partly visible. The typical extreme is nearer 10 hours
than 4 hours, and the typical magnitude has stopped growing
by 18 hours. The late window is a tail, not the center.

This is still not a hold, a target, or a stop.
A target near the median 24-hour extreme would sit on top of
a median adverse excursion of the same size.
